Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "How much does the definition matter? Concurrent drought-pluvial regimes and the occurrence of tornadoes, hail, heat waves, and lightning over the contiguous United States," by Nicholas J. Sokol, for publication in Natural Hazards. The paper falls within the journal's atmospheric and climatological hazard sections, as named in its aims and scope.

First, the manuscript decomposes the sensitivity of concurrent drought-pluvial event (CDPE) detection across a 756-configuration ensemble spanning the full index-construction chain, from index product and fitting distribution to detection threshold, buffer radius, and land-mask treatment. The threshold and the fitting distribution dominate CDPE frequency, configurations agree far less on event timing than on counts, and a land mask removes about 70 percent of baseline events.

Second, it conditions tornadoes, hail, heat waves, and lightning (the last over a shorter satellite record) on CDPE regime months, against calendar-month-matched non-CDPE months, under robustness rules fixed before the analysis ran. At the baseline configuration of 46 CDPE months, no association passes those rules, and the heat-wave estimate changes sign across the envelope. Each association is reported at the baseline and as an envelope across configurations, so readers can separate properties of the climate from properties of one catalog. That distinction is what impact studies, seasonal outlooks, and risk assessments built on such catalogs inherit. The convective elevation is sign-stable across configurations but small, and that negative knowledge keeps fragile associations out of downstream models.

Two related manuscripts are disclosed for transparency. The companion study that introduced the CDPE detector and the national catalog is under review at Physical Geography, and a regional analysis for the Southeastern United States is in revision at Southeastern Geographer. The enclosed manuscript is distinct from both, with no duplicated text or results.
