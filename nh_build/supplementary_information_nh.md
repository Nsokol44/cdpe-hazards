Supplementary Information S1. Comparison with the companion study's CDPE counts

Nicholas J. Sokol

Department of Geography & Sustainability, University of Tennessee, Knoxville, TN, USA

Supplementary Information for the manuscript "How much does the definition matter? Concurrent drought-pluvial regimes and the occurrence of tornadoes, hail, and heat waves over the contiguous United States," submitted to Natural Hazards. Section 3.1 of the main text summarizes this comparison and points here for the full detail.

At the baseline scenario (SPEI-1, theta = 1.5, r = 4 degrees), Arm A identifies 46 CDPE months over 1980 to 2023, against 52 reported by the companion study over 1980 to 2024 (Sokol 2026a). Every directly comparable count in Arm A is at or below the companion value (Table 2 of the main text), and the shortfall is not a property of our detector, since the companion study's original code, run unmodified on the same staged files, returns exactly our month sets (Jaccard 1.000 in all four comparisons).

The origin of the shortfall is not resolved. Both studies use the same SPEIbase release (v2.10), so a difference between releases is ruled out. Because the companion study's original code, run on the staged files used here, returns exactly our month sets, the difference lies in the inputs or run settings behind the companion study's reported counts, which we could not reconstruct. The companion SPI-1 count (66 months) likewise does not replicate under any construction variant tested (22 months at the documented specification). The qualitative conclusion is common to both studies: the SPI and SPEI catalogs are nearly disjoint (Jaccard 0.063 here, 0.054 in the companion study).
