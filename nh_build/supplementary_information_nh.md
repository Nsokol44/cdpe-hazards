Supplementary Information for the manuscript "How much does the definition matter? Concurrent drought-pluvial regimes and the occurrence of tornadoes, hail, heat waves, and lightning over the contiguous United States," submitted to Natural Hazards. Section 3.1 of the main text summarises this comparison and points here for the full detail.

At the baseline scenario (SPEI-1, theta = 1.5, r = 4 degrees), Arm A identifies 46 CDPE months over 1980 to 2023, against 52 reported by the companion study over 1980 to 2024 (Sokol 2026a). Every directly comparable count in Arm A is at or below the companion value (Table 2 of the main text), and the shortfall is not a property of our detector: the companion study's original code, run unmodified on the same staged files, returns exactly our month sets (Jaccard 1.000 in all four comparisons).

The shortfall therefore originates in the input fields, with a difference between SPEIbase releases the leading but unproven explanation, and the companion SPI-1 count (66 months) does not replicate under any construction variant tested (22 months at the documented specification). The qualitative conclusion is common to both studies: the SPI and SPEI catalogs are nearly disjoint (Jaccard 0.063 here, 0.054 in the companion study).
